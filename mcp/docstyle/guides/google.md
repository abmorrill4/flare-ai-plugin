# Google developer documentation style guide (baseline)

Condensed from the Google developer documentation style guide, https://developers.google.com/style,
licensed CC BY 4.0. This is a working summary for reviewers; the linked pages are authoritative.
The company guide is layered on top and wins on any conflict.

## Voice and tone
- Be conversational and friendly without being frivolous; be respectful and direct. https://developers.google.com/style/tone
- Avoid filler and hedges ("please", "simply", "just", "easily", "note that"); state the point. https://developers.google.com/style/tone
- Don't use exclamation points, and avoid hype or excessive claims. https://developers.google.com/style/tone

## Person and voice
- Address the reader as "you" (second person); avoid "the user", "we", and "let's". https://developers.google.com/style/person
- Prefer active voice; make clear who does the action. https://developers.google.com/style/voice
- Use present tense; avoid "will" for what the product does now. https://developers.google.com/style/tense

## Language and grammar
- Use plain, precise words; avoid jargon, slang, and idioms that don't translate. https://developers.google.com/style/jargon
- Keep sentences short, one idea each; put conditions before instructions ("To do X, click Y"). https://developers.google.com/style/clarity
- Write "for example" not "e.g.", "that is" not "i.e."; avoid "etc." and other Latin abbreviations. https://developers.google.com/style/abbreviations
- Use the serial (Oxford) comma. https://developers.google.com/style/commas
- Spell out abbreviations on first use unless they are better known abbreviated. https://developers.google.com/style/abbreviations
- Use standard American spelling; follow the word list for preferred terms. https://developers.google.com/style/word-list

## Procedures
- Use numbered lists for sequential steps; one action per step. https://developers.google.com/style/procedures
- Start each step with an imperative verb; state the location before the action ("In Settings, click Save"). https://developers.google.com/style/procedures
- Introduce a procedure with a sentence that says what it achieves. https://developers.google.com/style/procedures

## UI elements and interaction
- Bold UI element names and match their capitalization exactly. https://developers.google.com/style/ui-elements
- Use "click" for buttons, "select" for options and menus, "enter" for typed input; avoid "hit" and "press" for UI controls. https://developers.google.com/style/ui-elements
- Don't use directional language that depends on layout ("above", "the button on the right") as the only cue. https://developers.google.com/style/accessibility

## Headings and titles
- Use sentence case. https://developers.google.com/style/capitalization
- Task headings start with a bare infinitive ("Configure single sign-on"); conceptual headings use noun phrases. https://developers.google.com/style/headings
- No end punctuation in headings except question marks. https://developers.google.com/style/headings

## Links
- Use descriptive link text that makes sense out of context; never "click here" or "this link". https://developers.google.com/style/link-text

## Inclusive and accessible language
- Use inclusive terms: "allowlist/blocklist", "primary/replica"; avoid ableist and gendered language. https://developers.google.com/style/inclusive-documentation
- Don't rely on color or images alone to convey meaning. https://developers.google.com/style/accessibility

## Numbers, dates, and units
- Spell out zero through nine in body text; use numerals for 10 and above, and for measurements, versions, and UI values. https://developers.google.com/style/numbers
- Use unambiguous date formats ("January 19, 2026"). https://developers.google.com/style/dates-times
- Put a nonbreaking space between a number and its unit ("10 GB"). https://developers.google.com/style/units-of-measure
