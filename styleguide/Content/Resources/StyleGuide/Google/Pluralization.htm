Pluralization

  • Follow the standard rules for pluralization in US English and use the regular plural form of a word in most cases.
  • Avoid using 's to form a plural to avoid confusing a plural with a possessive or contraction.

Singular and plural

  • For sentences with long or complex subjects, make sure to use either a plural or singular appropriately.
    • Bad: The efficiency of algorithms that process data sets depend on memory allocation.
    • Good: Confirm that the number of entries listed in the directory is accurate.
  • For sentences with more than one subject connected by and or or, make sure to use either a plural or singular appropriately.
    • Bad: User authentication and authorization is processed and handled by the security module.
    • Good: The request payload and header information are logged for debugging.
  • For consistent style, use a plural after one or more, not a singular.
    • Good: If one or more tests fail, a system warning is triggered.
  • Because one or more can express the possibility of one or more outcomes, reword the sentence for clarity when it helps.
    • Good: If any one test fails, a system warning is triggered.
  • Use a singular after more than one, not a plural.
    • Good: You can create more than one instance at a time.

Plural abbreviations

  • Treat acronyms, initialisms, and other abbreviations as regular words when making them plural, and avoid using 's to form the plural.
    • Bad: API's, SKE's, and IDE's
    • Good: APIs, SKEs, and IDEs
  • If the acronym, initialism, or abbreviation ends in s, sh, ch, or x, then add es—for example, OSes, DISHes, DCCHes, and BMXes.
    • Good: OSes, DISHes, DCCHes, and BMXes
  • When spelling out a term, make sure that both the spelled-out term and abbreviation match, with both either being a plural or singular.
    • Bad: virtual machines (VM)
    • Good: virtual machines (VMs)
  • When spelling out a unit of measure with a number, use the singular if the number is one, and the plural for all other numbers, including zero, decimal numbers, and numbers greater than one.
    • Good: 0.5 degrees

Plural product and feature names

  • Don't form a plural or possessive for the trademark of a product, feature, or company name.
  • Use singular class names; don't manually make a singular class name plural—instead, add a plural noun after the class name.
    • Bad: Intents and Activities
    • Good: Intent objects and Activity instances

Plurals in parentheses

  • Use either a plural or singular construction and keep things consistent throughout your documentation, choosing what is most appropriate for your documentation and your audience.
    • Bad: The value of the parent depends on the value(s) of its child(ren).
    • Good: The value of the parent depends on the values of its children.
  • If it's important in a specific context to indicate both singular and plural, use one or more.
    • Bad: You can use a physical linecard, which can contain port(s).
    • Good: You can use a physical linecard, which can contain one or more ports.
