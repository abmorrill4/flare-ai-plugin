Hyphens

  • Use a hyphen when needed for clarity, to separate parts of words to avoid misreadings or to combine terms that should be read as a unit.

General guidelines

  • If there's an established convention for hyphenating a term in the documentation set that you're working with, follow that convention.
  • If you're not sure whether to hyphenate a term, check the word list in this style guide, and then the Merriam-Webster dictionary.

Prefixes

  • In general, don't use a hyphen between a prefix and the main noun.
    • Good: infrastructure, megabyte, metadata, preprocessing, pseudocode, semiconductor

Exceptions

  • Add a hyphen after a prefix if the prefix is self or cross.
    • Good: self-managing, cross-region
  • Add a hyphen after a prefix if the noun is capitalized or is a number.
    • Good: non-Google, post-2000
  • Add a hyphen after a prefix to avoid confusion or difficulty in reading.
    • Good: de-energize, intra-index, re-mark, re-sign
  • Add a hyphen after a prefix if the prefix is for a term that already has hyphens or spaces.
    • Good: un-Google-like, non-twentieth-century
  • Add a hyphen after a prefix to be consistent within a document.
    • Good: pre-processing, post-processing
  • Follow the word list for well-established terms that commonly use a hyphen or a space, such as multi-region and style sheet, including where noun, verb, and adjective versions are treated differently.
    • Good: multi-region, style sheet
  • When the components of a unit of measurement are multiplied by each other, hyphenate them.
    • Good: 5 vCPU-hours
  • Don't hyphenate adverbs that end in -ly except when needed for clarity.
    • Bad: Publicly-available implementations
    • Good: Publicly available implementations
  • Don't use hyphens in compounds that are conventionally not hyphenated; follow the word list or the convention in the documentation that you're working with.
    • Good: A machine learning model
  • Hyphenate compound terms that are always hyphenated even if they follow a verb, checking the word list, then the Merriam-Webster dictionary, and the convention in your documentation.
    • Good: You can deploy the app on-premises.

The non prefix

  • For the non prefix, use your best judgment about hyphenating, because it can easily form words that are hard to parse, taking into account consistency within your documentation.
    • Good: noncurrent, nonempty, noninteractive, nonpublic; non-existence, non-integer, non-key, non-managed, non-negative
  • When using non as a prefix, add a hyphen before hyphenated compound words.
    • Good: non-KSA-based, non-self-sustaining

Compound nouns

  • In general, write compound nouns in their closed (one-word, unhyphenated) form, including when the closed form is the predominant convention in your context even if Merriam-Webster uses another form.
    • Good: webpage

Compound modifiers before a noun

  • If needed for clarity, hyphenate compound modifiers that come before a noun.
    • Good: A well-designed app
  • Use a hyphen after more or most if you need to clarify what those words modify.
    • Good: Edge locations with more-reliable internet links
  • In general, avoid writing compound modifiers that have more than two words; instead, move some words after the noun.
    • Bad: edition-2023-specific test cases
    • Good: test cases that are specific to the 2023 edition
  • If you must use a compound modifier of more than two words, use a hyphen between each word as needed for clarity.
    • Good: cross-data-center replication

Numbers and units of measurement

  • Hyphenate a number and a spelled-out unit of measurement when they combine to modify a noun.
    • Good: a 64-bit system
  • Don't hyphenate if the unit of measurement is abbreviated unless the hyphen is needed for clarity; instead, use a nonbreaking space between the number and unit of measurement.
    • Good: 200 GB disk

Compound terms after a verb

  • In general, don't add a hyphen to a compound that follows a verb.
    • Good: The app is well designed.

Range of numbers

  • Use a hyphen, not an en dash, to indicate a range of numbers.
    • Good: 8-20 files
  • If a hyphen in a range of numbers introduces ambiguity, use words such as from, to, and through for clarity.
    • Good: from 8 to 20 files

(?<=\d)\u2013(?=\d)-suggestionRanges of numbers use a hyphen.Upload 8–20 files.
\b[Ff]rom \d{1,4}\s?-\s?\d{1,4}\b(?!-)suggestionUse from…to, not from with a hyphen.Upload from 8-20 files.

Spaces around hyphens

  • Never place a space on either side of a hyphen except when using a suspended hyphen, in which case you can leave a space after (but not before) the hyphen.

(?<=[a-z]) - (?=[a-z])suggestionHyphens never have spaces on both sides.The app is fast - and secure.

Suspended hyphens

  • When two or more compound modifiers have a common base, you can keep the hyphens but leave out the base for all except the last modifier.
    • Good: You can set up the system to scan for new files at one- or two-hour intervals.
