Second person

Compiled from the Google developer documentation style guide, Second person, licensed CC BY 4.0. Generated: edit the company rules instead, and turn Google rules off or change them there.

Address the reader as you

  • Address the reader in the second person: use you or your instead of we, our, or us.
    • Bad: The following sections describe how we can create a website.
    • Good: The following sections describe how you can create a website.
  • Address the reader as you instead of using first-person plural constructions such as Let's and our.
    • Bad: Let's add a description to our table.
    • Good: Consider adding a description to your table.
  • Assume that the reader is the person who's doing the tasks or making the decisions.
  • Use the word user only to refer to the user of the software that your reader is developing.
    • Bad: This document shows the user how to develop an app for their organization.
    • Good: This document shows you how to develop an app for your organization.
  • If you're telling the reader to do something, then use the imperative (the you is implied).
    • Good: Click Submit.
  • Use the imperative in running text only after you establish who is being addressed.
    • Good: You can obtain the IP address for the appliance from your network administrator. Store the address in a variable for future use in the runbook.
  • Consider whether imperative text in running text needs to be formatted as a procedure.
    • Bad: To hold the backup data, create a storage bucket. In the Google Cloud console, go to the Buckets page. Click Create bucket.
  • Use the second person to address what the reader does, but use the third person for what the software or an end user does.
  • In API documentation, use the third person to state facts about programming elements, but address the reader as you when you tell them what to do with them.

Use first-person plural pronouns carefully

  • Use first-person plural pronouns (such as we, our, or us) only to refer to the organization that's represented as the author of the document.
    • Good: For more information, contact our sales organization.
  • When you use we, our, or us for the authoring organization, ensure that the antecedent for the pronoun is clear.
    • Good: Example Organization provides A and B, but we don't provide C and D.

Address your audience consistently

  • Identify who the you is that you're addressing (a developer, a sysadmin, someone else) and be consistent about that.
  • Make it clear to the reader who you expect them to be, sometimes with an explicit audience sentence near the beginning of the document.
