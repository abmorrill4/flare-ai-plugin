Units of measurement

Spaces in units of measurement

  • For most units of measurement, when you specify a number with the unit, use a nonbreaking space between the number and the unit.
    • Bad: 64GB
    • Good: 64 GB
  • When the unit of measure is money or percent or degrees of an angle, don't use a space.
    • Good: $10
  • For degrees of temperature, include a nonbreaking space between the number and the degree symbol, and don't use a space between the degree symbol and the temperature scale (F or C).
    • Good: 50 °C

Markdown

  • For Kelvin temperatures, leave out the degree symbol but use a nonbreaking space before the K.
    • Good: 300 K
  • When a number and unit of measurement combine to modify a noun, don't hyphenate unless the hyphen is needed for clarity.
    • Good: 200 GB disk
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\d[ \u00A0]?°[ \u00A0]?K\bsuggestionKelvin temperatures don't use a degree symbol.The sample is cooled to 300 °K.

Ranges of numbers with units

  • In a range of numbers, repeat the unit for each number; unit includes symbols and abbreviations but not nouns.
    • Bad: -40-85 °C
    • Good: -40 °C to 85 °C
  • Use the word to between the numbers in a range, rather than a hyphen.
    • Bad: -40-85 °C
    • Good: -40 °C to 85 °C
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\d+[-–]\d+[ \u00A0]?(?:°[CF]|kB|KB|MB|GB|TB|KiB|MiB|GiB|TiB|%)suggestionA hyphen can be misinterpreted as a subtraction sign.The operating range is -40-85 °C.

Hyphens with multiplied units

  • When the components of a unit of measurement are multiplied by each other, hyphenate them.
    • Good: 5 vCPU-hours

Use k to indicate thousands

  • If you indicate thousands with a lowercase k, add a noun to indicate what the number measures, to make clear that you're not using k as an abbreviation for kilobytes.
    • Good: On this plan, you are limited to 55k download operations and 20k upload operations per day.
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
(?<=\d)[ \u00A0]k\bsuggestionYou are limited to 55 k download operations.

Currency

  • If you're writing about monetary amounts, make sure that the reader knows what currency you're referring to.
  • If there's any possibility of ambiguity, use a currency indicator before the amount.
    • Good: US$10

Rates

  • Use per instead of the division slash (/) when space permits; it's OK to use the slash when space is limited, such as in a table with small cells.
    • Bad: requests/day
    • Good: requests per day
  • Shorten per to p only for well-established abbreviations for rate units, such as Gbps for gigabits per second or MBps for megabytes per second.
    • Bad: Gb/s
    • Good: Gbps
AvoidUseSeverityWhyExample
Gb/sGbpswarningThe link supports 10 Gb/s.

Decimal and binary units

  • Use the same system to measure bytes as the technology that you're documenting; don't use MB if you mean MiB, or GB if you mean GiB.
