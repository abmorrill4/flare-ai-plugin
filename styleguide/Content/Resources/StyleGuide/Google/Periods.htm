Periods and end punctuation

  • End a complete sentence with a period, unless it's a question; lists have exceptions.

Periods with URLs

  • Whenever possible, avoid putting URLs in text.
  • Rewrite the sentence so that the URL isn't at the end of the sentence.
  • Put the URL on a separate line from the text, omitting the final period.
    • Bad: We use your feedback to improve the Animals API, in accordance with Example Pet Store's Privacy Policy at http://www.examplepetstore.com/privacy/.
    • Good: We use your feedback to improve the Animals API, in accordance with Example Pet Store's Privacy Policy: http://www.examplepetstore.com/privacy/
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
https?://\S+[ \t]+\.(?=\s|$)errorSee the policy at http://www.example.com/privacy/ .

Periods with quotation marks

  • When a sentence ends with material inside quotation marks, place the period inside the quotation marks, except when the quotation marks enclose a keyword or other literal string.
    • Good: ... you might say "Fixed typo."
  • If the material inside the quotation marks ends with a question mark or an exclamation point, don't use a period.
    • Good: Children always ask "Why?"
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
[?!]["”]\.errorChildren always ask "Why?".

Periods with parentheses

  • If the last part of a sentence is contained inside parentheses, put the period after the closing parenthesis.
    • Good: Your application could show a notification when a relevant file or folder has changed (even if that change occurs while your application isn't running).
  • If the parentheses contain a complete sentence, put the period inside the parentheses.
    • Good: App Engine applications are easy to create, easy to maintain, and easy to scale. (With App Engine, there are no servers for you to maintain.)

Periods with headings

  • Don't end headings with periods.

Periods with numbers

  • Use a period to represent a decimal point, not a comma.

Periods with abbreviations

  • Put a period after a shortened word.

Exclamation points

  • In general, avoid exclamation points.
  • Never use exclamation points in concept and reference docs; maintain a neutral, objective tone.
  • In procedural topics, avoid exclamation points and use periods for completion steps.
    • Good: The VM is created.
  • In code examples, use exclamation points when required by syntax, such as the != operator.
  • Use an exclamation point when it's part of a specific error code or log message that must be matched exactly.
  • In tutorials and learning modules, use exclamation points sparingly to mark major milestones or achievements.
    • Good: Congratulations! You've completed the setup.
