Abbreviations

Compiled from the Google developer documentation style guide, Abbreviations, licensed CC BY 4.0. Generated: edit the company rules instead, and turn Google rules off or change them there.

Long and short versions of a word

  • Don't put a period after the short version of a word, such as app, demo, or sync; if you speak the short version as a word, treat it as a word and not an abbreviation.
    • Good: This is a demo version of the product

General dos and don'ts

  • Use standard acronyms and initialisms that will save the reader time.
  • Avoid using abbreviations for terms that aren't related to the main topic of the document.
    • Bad: The IoT (internet of things) service can even be used for connecting to sensors in LEO (low Earth orbit).
    • Good: The internet of things (IoT) service can even be used for connecting to sensors in low Earth orbit.
  • Be wary of using specialized abbreviations that your readers might not understand.

When to spell out a term

  • Spell out abbreviations on first reference: when an abbreviation is likely to be unfamiliar to the audience, spell it out upon first mention and include the abbreviation in parentheses immediately following.
    • Good: Border Gateway Protocol (BGP)
  • For all subsequent mentions of the abbreviation, use the abbreviation by itself.
  • If you use an abbreviation only once, include it only if you think the abbreviation is as commonly used as the spelled-out term; otherwise, don't include the abbreviation.
  • If the first mention of a term occurs in a heading or title, you can use the abbreviation and then spell out the abbreviation in the first paragraph that follows the heading or title.
  • When deciding to spell out a term, consider your audience: if the majority of your audience is likely to recognize and understand the term, you don't need to spell it out.
  • Don't spell out a term when spelling it out doesn't help the reader understand it; abbreviations such as API, DVD, file formats like PDF or XML, HTML, RAM, REST, units of measurement like MB, MiB, GB, or GiB, URL, and USB rarely need to be spelled out.

Format abbreviation introductions

  • When you spell out a term and include its abbreviation in parentheses, italicize both the spelled-out term and its abbreviation.
  • Capitalize the spelled-out version of the abbreviation only if the long form is a proper noun or is conventionally capitalized; don't capitalize the term only because the abbreviation includes capital letters.
    • Bad: Data Manipulation Language (DML)
    • Good: data manipulation language (DML)
  • Include the abbreviation in link text.

Abbreviations not to use

  • It's okay to use etc. in some circumstances, but it's best to use different phrasing in most lists.
  • Don't use internet slang abbreviations; write out what you mean in a non-figurative way.
  • Use the most common form of a word: if the spelled-out word is common and easily understandable, use that rather than abbreviating.
  • Spell out shortened words or common symbols that are substitutions for words.
    • Bad: Updating the software made throughput 10x faster.
    • Good: Updating the software made throughput 10 times faster.

Periods with abbreviations

  • Don't use periods with acronyms or initialisms.
  • Put a period at the end of a shortened word, except for date and time abbreviations.

U.S., U.K., D.C.US, UK, DCwarningThe service is available in the U.S. only.

Abbreviations as verbs

  • Don't use acronyms, initialisms, or shortened words as verbs.
    • Bad: Then ssh into your remote shell.
    • Good: Use SSH to log in to your remote shell.

Indefinite articles before abbreviations

  • Use a before any consonant sound and an before any vowel sound, basing your decision on the pronunciation of the abbreviation that's most common for your audience.
