Numbers

Compiled from the Google developer documentation style guide, Numbers, licensed CC BY 4.0. Generated: edit the company rules instead, and turn Google rules off or change them there.

Ordinal numbers

  • Spell out all ordinal numbers in text.
    • Bad: 1st, 5th, 12th, 43rd
    • Good: first, fifth, twelfth, forty-third
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\b\d+(?:st|nd|rd|th)\bwarningOrdinal numbers are spelled out in text.Select the 1st option in the list.

Numbers as words

  • If it's important to have the number and associated noun together on the same line, use a nonbreaking space between the number and the noun.
  • Spell out numbers from zero through nine, except as noted in Numbers as numerals.
    • Good: four options
  • Spell out a number that starts a sentence; it's okay, but non-optimal, to begin a sentence with a four-digit year.
    • Good: Fifteen directories are created.
  • In some cases, rearrange the sentence so that the number appears later instead of starting the sentence.
    • Bad: 164 MB is generally considered too large a file to send as an attachment.
    • Good: In general, avoid sending files larger than 164 MB as attachments.
  • Spell out a number that is followed by a numeral, or rephrase so both are numerals.
    • Good: This procedure creates fifteen 100,000-byte files.
  • Use words like millions or billions only for approximate numbers; for precise numbers, use numerals.
    • Good: You can specify thousands of combinations.

Numbers as numerals

  • Use numerals for numbers 10 and greater.
  • Use numerals for version numbers.
    • Good: version 3
  • Use numerals for technical quantities, such as amounts of memory, amounts of disk space, numbers of queries, or usage limits.
    • Good: 6 queries per second
  • Use numerals for page numbers, chapter numbers, sections, and so on.
  • Avoid referring to step numbers whenever possible, but when you must, use the numeral.
  • Use numerals for prices.
  • Use numerals for numbers without units, such as numbers used in mathematical expressions.
  • Use numerals for numbers less than 10 when they appear in the same sentence with numbers greater than 9.
    • Good: The menu contains 15 options but 6 of them are deselected.
  • Use numerals for negative numbers.
  • Use numerals for most fractions.
  • Use numerals for percentages.
  • Use numerals for numbers containing decimal points.
  • Treat decimal numbers as plural even when less than or equal to 1.0.
    • Good: 0.3 inches
  • For decimal numbers less than one, place a zero in front of the decimal point.
    • Good: 0.3 inches
  • Use numerals for measurements.
    • Good: 8 pixels
  • Use numerals for numbers in a range.
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
(?<=^|\s)\.\d+\bwarningDecimals less than one need a leading zero.Set the margin to .3 inches.

Numbers as Roman numerals

  • Avoid using Roman numerals when possible; instead, use Arabic numerals because they are easier to scan.
  • You can use Roman numerals for sub-steps in numbered procedures.

Fractions

  • Express fractions as decimal numbers, when possible.
    • Good: 0.75
  • If you must express fractions as words, connect the numerator and denominator with a hyphen unless one of them is already hyphenated.
    • Good: two-fifths

Percentages

  • In general, use numerals and the percent sign (%), without a space between them.
    • Good: 40%
  • If the percentage starts the sentence, then spell out both the number and the word percent.
    • Good: Forty percent of the files
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\b\d+(?:\.\d+)?\s+%warningNo space between numeral and percent sign.Usage rose to 40 % this month.
\b\d+(?:\.\d+)?[ \u00A0]?percent\bwarningUse numerals with the percent sign.Only 40 percent of the files were copied.

Ranges of numbers

  • In ranges of numbers, use a hyphen with no space on either side of it; do not use an en dash.
    • Good: 2012-2016
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\d\s*(?:–|&ndash;)\s*\dwarningRanges use a hyphen, not an en dash.The project ran from 2012–2016.

Suspended hyphens

  • When two or more hyphenated compounds that start with numbers modify the same word, use suspended hyphens.
    • Good: You can set up the system to scan for new files at one-, two-, or three-hour intervals.

Currency

  • Make sure that it's clear what country's currency you are describing.
  • For US dollars, use a comma to delineate the thousands place of whole currency.
    • Bad: $10 000 in fees is out of reach for many developers.
    • Good: $10,000 in fees is out of reach for many developers.
  • For US dollars, use a period to delineate whole currency and fractions of currency.
  • For US dollars, always include the dollar sign ($) at the beginning of the currency.
  • For US dollars, do not use any punctuation or spaces to the right of the decimal.
    • Bad: The price is $0.006,653 per vCPU hour.
    • Good: The price is $0.006653 per vCPU hour.
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\$\d{1,3}(?: \d{3})+\bwarningUse commas to separate thousands in US dollars.$10 000 in fees is out of reach for many developers.
\$\d[\d,]*\.\d+(?:[, ]\d{3})+\berrorNo digit separators right of the decimal point.The price is $0.006,653 per vCPU hour.

Commas and decimal points in numbers

  • Use commas and decimal points in accordance with standard American number-formatting.
  • In numbers four or more digits long, use commas to set off groups of three digits, counting leftward from the decimal point.
    • Bad: The limit is 1532784 bytes per day.
    • Good: The limit is 1,532,784 bytes per day.
  • For long decimal numbers, do not use any digit-group separators to the right of the decimal point.
    • Bad: $0.031 611/vCPU hour
    • Good: $0.031611/vCPU hour
  • Use a period for a decimal point, in the standard American style.

Dimensions

  • Use numerals for dimensions.
    • Good: 192x192
  • Use a lowercase x between the numerals in the dimensions, with no space between the numerals and the x.
    • Bad: 192 x 192
    • Good: 192x192
PatternUseSeverityWhyExample
\b\d+(?: [xX×] ?| ?[xX×] |X|×)\d+\bwarningDimensions use a lowercase x with no spaces.The icon must be 192 x 192 pixels.

Exponents

  • Use standard mathematical notation for exponents.
  • Don't put a space between the base and the exponent.
    • Good: 2³

Accompany numerical concepts with real-world practical implications

  • Accompany numerical concepts with real-world practical implications to provide tangible meaning; for example, if using a feature incurs additional fees, add a link to pricing calculator.
