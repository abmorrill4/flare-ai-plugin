Paragraphs

  • Break up your paragraphs to aid in the scannability of the page and to avoid walls of text.
  • Make each paragraph address a single idea in the fewest words and in the fewest sentences possible.
  • Don't make sentences longer in order to limit the number of sentences in a paragraph; use shorter sentences and paragraphs.
  • If a paragraph longer than 5 or 6 sentences is trying to convey too much information, break the paragraph into smaller paragraphs or remove some content.
  • Don't break paragraphs up if they contain a single idea; a one-sentence paragraph, or one longer than 6 sentences about one idea, is OK.

Put critical information first

  • Put the most important information first in a paragraph; don't hide the key point of a paragraph at the end of the paragraph.

Format paragraphs

  • Left-align text for readability; don't center, full-justify, or right-align text.
  • Don't force line breaks (hard returns) within sentences and paragraphs.
