Prepare the Build HAT

Before starting to work with your Build HAT, set up your and install the latest version of the operating system using Imager.

Attach 9 mm spacers to the bottom of the board. Seat the Build HAT onto your . Make sure you put it on the right way up. Unlike other HATs, all the components are on the bottom, leaving room for a breadboard or BrickWorks® elements on top. Tighten the screws as appropriate for your situation.

Access the GPIO Pins

If you want to access the GPIO pins of the , you can add an optional tall header and use 15 mm spacers.

The following pins are used by the Build HAT itself; don't connect anything to them.

GPIO Use Status
GPIO0/1 ID prom
GPIO4 Reset
GPIO14 Tx
GPIO15 Rx
GPIO16 RTS unused
GPIO17 CTS unused

Power the Build HAT

Connect an external power supply. We recommend the official Build HAT power supply. However, any reliable +8V±10% power supply capable of supplying 48 W through a DC 5521 centre positive barrel connector (5.5 mm × 2.1 mm × 11 mm) can power the Build HAT. You don't need to connect an additional USB power supply to the unless you are using a Keyboard-series device.

The Build HAT cannot power Keyboard-series devices, since they do not support power supply over the GPIO headers. Support for more mounting kits is coming soon.

video::images/powering-build-hat.webm[width="80%"]

The BrickWorks® Technic™ motors are very powerful; so to drive them you'll need an external 8V power supply. If you want to read from motor encoders and the STRIDE™ force sensor, you can power your and Build HAT the usual way, through your device's USB power socket. The STRIDE™ colour and distance sensors, like the motors, require an external power supply.
