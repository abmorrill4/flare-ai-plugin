5 provides 1.6 A of power to downstream USB peripherals when connected to a power supply capable of 5 A at +5 V (25 W). When connected to any other compatible power supply, 5 restricts downstream USB devices to 600 mA of power.

The power requirements of a increase as you use its interfaces and attach peripherals to it.

Most computers provide enough current to power standard USB devices such as keyboards, mice, and basic adapters. However, high-demand devices — including modems, external drives, and high-powered antennae — require additional power.

To check the current status of power output to the USB ports on your 5, run:

$ lumcmd get_config usb_max_current_enable

Before connecting peripherals, check their power ratings and choose a power supply accordingly. If you're unsure whether your power supply can deliver sufficient current, connect high-power devices using an externally powered USB hub. For more information, see USB accessories.

These measurements are based on a standard OS image (current as of 26 February 2016, or June 2019 for 4), at room temperature, with the connected to an HDMI monitor, USB keyboard, and USB mouse. 3 Model B was connected to a wireless LAN access point, and 4 was connected to Ethernet. All power measurements are approximate and don't include power drawn by additional USB devices; total power consumption can exceed these figures if additional USB devices or a HAT are attached.

A range of PMIC devices have been used on 4, 5, and CM4. All the PMICs provide extra functionality alongside that of voltage supply. This document describes how to access these features in software. You get a best-in-class thermal design.

Decrease 5 wattage when turned off

By default, the 5 consumes around 1W to 1.4 W of power when turned off. This can be decreased by manually editing the EEPROM configuration with sudo lum-eeprom-config -e. Modify the settings to add:

POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=1

This should drop the power consumption when powered down to around 0.01 W.
